# SEO strategy for timothygaull.com

Last updated: October 2026. Research and recommendations only. No site files were changed.

**Read this first.** There was no keyword tool and no Search Console data. Every demand and competition figure here is an **estimate** built from public signals: Google autocomplete, Bing autocomplete, who shows up in search results, and what owners write in forums. None of it is real search volume. Section 10 says how to replace the estimates with real numbers.

How the labels work:

| Label | High | Med | Low |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Demand (est.)** | Autocomplete shows 8 to 10 related variants, and the results are crowded with big brands or vendors | A few variants, mixed results | The phrase barely autocompletes, or not at all |
| **Competition (est.)** | National brands, VC-backed software vendors, big agencies, or an established local map pack | Mid-size content sites, individual consultants, programmatic city pages | Forums, thin blogs, contest sites, few direct answers |
| **Confidence** | Several signals agree | Some signals | Mostly inference |

Intent: **Learn** (wants to understand), **Compare** (weighing options or price), **Hire** (looking for someone). Scope: **Local** (Nashville and Middle Tennessee) or **National**.

## 1. One-screen summary

**The position to own in search:** the hands-on AI and business systems consultant in Nashville for owner-led companies, especially trades, fabrication and other industrial and service businesses. He writes down how the business really runs before he automates anything, and he shows real, named work.

The broad national terms ("AI consultant", "AI consulting for small business") belong to software firms and big content sites. A new site can't win them soon. Tim wins by being specific: **owner-led + Middle Tennessee + trades and industrial + Claude-native + real case studies.** Almost nobody ranking locally today has named case studies. The Nashville AI pages that rank are programmatic city pages, listicles and generic agency blogs.

**Lead with the AI Opportunity Sprint.** It matches the most hiring-intent searches ("AI consultant Nashville", "AI consulting for small business", "AI implementation consultant for small business", "AI audit"). It is already the "start here" offer, and the free kit is literally its first step. Fractional AI & Growth Partner comes second: "fractional chief AI officer" is growing and individual consultants rank for it. Brand & Business Foundations comes third for search, because local brand searches are crowded with agencies. One brand exception is wide open nationally: "how to create a brand identity with AI / with Claude". The Gunter Metals case study already fits it.

### The 5 highest-value next steps

1. **Make sure search engines can find the site at all.** In testing with the research search tool (not Google), searches for "Timothy Gaull" and "Gaull & Co" did not return the site, and "Gunter Metals" returned neither the case study nor guntermetals.com. Set up Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, submit the sitemap, and request indexing for every page. Turn on Cloudflare Crawler Hints (IndexNow). This takes an hour and everything else depends on it.
2. **Finish the Gaull & Co Google Business Profile and copy it to Bing Places and Apple Business Connect.** Primary category "Business management consultant", service areas across Middle Tennessee, all three services listed, and the first 5 to 10 real client reviews. ChatGPT's local answers lean heavily on Bing data, so Bing Places matters as much as Google.
3. **Build the pages that can rank.** Today the three services exist only as cards on the home page, with no URL of their own. Add `/ai-consulting/`, `/fractional-ai/`, `/brand-strategy/` and one strong `/nashville/` page. Without these, there is nothing for "AI consultant Nashville" or "fractional AI" to land on.
4. **Fix titles, descriptions and case study schema** (section 9). Every case study title runs 68 to 90 characters with the name added, and every description runs 183 to 296. Add dates, images and breadcrumbs to the case study schema. Retitle Gunter Metals around "AI-designed brand identity".
5. **Publish the next case study with the playbook in section 7, aimed at AI for trades: quoting, intake triage or lead capture.** Only if there is real client work to show. The kit page's before/after panel already describes a real fabrication-shop lead-capture project, anonymized until the client approves naming.

**Realistic timing.** Local map results can move in 1 to 3 months once the profile, reviews and a Nashville page exist. National long-tail searches usually take 3 to 9 months for a new site. National head terms are out of reach for at least a year, and maybe for good.

## 2. What the research found

- **Local AI search is thin and generic.** "ai consultant nashville" autocompletes, but suburb versions (Franklin, Brentwood, Murfreesboro) and "ai agency / ai automation nashville" return no suggestions. Bing returns no suggestions for any Nashville service term. Results are programmatic pages (Zfort, CodeWCG neighborhood pages), listicles (AI Superior, The Manifest, Clutch), an IT support firm, and agency blogs written from Jackson, TN. None show named local client work. A real local practitioner with case studies and reviews can stand out.
- **"Near me" searches exist for every service.** "ai consultant for small business near me", "brand strategist near me", "branding consultant near me", "small business consultant near me" and "construction business coach near me" all autocomplete. These are decided by the map pack, so the Business Profile does most of the work.
- **"AI for [industry]" is owned by software vendors.** "ai for contractors", "ai for roofing companies", "ai for hvac business" and "ai for property management" autocomplete richly, but the variants are tools: receptionists, estimating software, apps. A consultant can't outrank tool vendors for those. A consultant can win the long tail ("AI consultant for construction", "how to use AI in a fabrication business") and owner-problem searches.
- **"Claude for [anything]" is a new opening.** Anthropic launched Claude for Small Business on May 13, 2026. "claude for small business" now has 10 autocomplete variants (training, skills, course, pricing, QuickBooks). "claude for real estate investors", "claude skills for business owners" and "how to create a brand identity with claude" all autocomplete. Results are thin blogs, Fiverr gigs and a few setup consultants. Anthropic's free workshop tour visited 10 cities and none were in Tennessee (closest: Birmingham). Tim already designs with Claude and ships a Claude Skill.
- **Individual consultants do rank nationally for long-tail AI questions.** lilachbullock.com, ryandoser.com, kamyarshah.com, bushe.co and alexgoryachev.com rank for "AI consultant cost", "what does an AI consultant do" and "fractional chief AI officer". It is winnable with clear, specific, first-hand pages.
- **Owners search problems in their own words.** "how to build systems so your business runs without you", "work on your business not in it", "how to systemize your business", "how to scale a construction business", "how to grow a metal fabrication business", "how to quote metal fabrication" and "how to train ai on my business" all autocomplete. Longer phrasings like "owner is the bottleneck" or "how to get out of the day to day" barely register as searches. They are still good language for headings, because people say them in conversation and AI chat prompts.
- **There is a Tennessee manufacturing echo.** The UT Center for Industrial Services (Tennessee's MEP) launched an "AI Opportunity Discovery Assessment" for manufacturers. That's a similar name to the Sprint, aimed at the same buyers. Treat it as a possible referral partner, and make the Sprint page say clearly how it differs (hands-on, first win set up live).
- **Local demand for AI help in trades is real.** A one-day "AI Summer Summit for Trades Businesses" ran at the Nashville Entrepreneur Center on July 24, 2026. Eventbrite lists AI workshops for Nashville business owners.

Research limits are in section 11.

## 6. AI answer-engine plan (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, Claude, Copilot)

What the evidence says:
- Google says there's no special trick for AI Overviews or AI Mode. Normal SEO, crawlability and helpful content are what count. ([Google: AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features))
- ChatGPT search leans on Bing's index and on Bing Places for local answers. Third-party lists and review sites matter. ([Whitespark](https://whitespark.ca/blog/want-to-rank-in-chatgpt-focus-on-these-review-sites-new-research/))
- `llms.txt` shows **no measurable citation lift** in one 2026 study of 2,400 domains, and AI crawlers rarely fetch it. Treat it as a cheap fact sheet, not a ranking lever. ([MaxAEO study](https://maxaeo.ai/blog/does-llms-txt-work/), a vendor study, so directional only)
- Vendor studies say cited small businesses tend to share four things: AI crawler access, schema, a clear entity profile, and consistent listings. Perplexity cites a small business's own site more often than ChatGPT does. ([ThinkProfits 50-query study](https://thinkprofits.com/digital-news/50-smb-queries-chatgpt-perplexity-google-ai-mode-citation-study), directional only)

What the site already does well: `robots.txt` explicitly allows GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and others. Person, ProfessionalService, WebSite, Article, ProfilePage, CreativeWork and FAQPage schema are present. `llms.txt` exists and is linked from every page.

What to add:
1. **Bing Webmaster Tools and IndexNow** (via Cloudflare Crawler Hints). This is the single most useful step for ChatGPT and Copilot visibility. Bing's 2026 AI Performance report shows Copilot citations. ([Cloudflare Crawler Hints](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cache/advanced-configuration/crawler-hints/))
2. **Entity consistency.** Add the Business Profile URL, LinkedIn company page and Clutch profile to `sameAs` on both the Person and the ProfessionalService in `src/layouts/Base.astro`. Add "Gaull & Co" and the service area to `public/llms.txt`; it currently never mentions the practice name.
3. **Write facts so they can be quoted.** One fact per sentence, with subject, verb, number, unit, place and date. Put the answer first, then the detail.
   - Weak: "The brand took four days and a lot of iteration."
   - Quotable: "Timothy Gaull designed the Gunter Metals brand identity in four days, using Claude as a design partner."
   - Weak: "The AI part only works because the business rules were written down first."
   - Quotable: "Gunter Metals' product and pricing rules live in plain, tested files that the website and AI shopping assistants both read."
4. **Use the `facts` block** on every case study for the 3 to 5 facts you'd want an AI to repeat (industry, location, service, timeline, result). It sits at the top of the page in a `<dl>`, which is easy to extract.
5. **Use question H2s with a 40 to 60 word direct answer** right under them ("Can AI design a brand identity?"). That's the shape AI Overviews and Perplexity lift.
6. **Freshness.** Add `datePublished` and `dateModified` to the Article schema and show "Published / Updated" on the page.
7. **Third-party corroboration.** AI engines trust what others say. Reviews, Clutch, Chamber listings, local press and "best AI consultants in Nashville" lists do more than anything on the site itself.
8. **Approved client quotes.** The `/feedback/` form already collects stories and gets approval on exact wording. A short, attributed owner quote ("What can you do now that you couldn't before?") is highly quotable.
9. **Monthly prompt test** (section 10). Ask each engine the same six questions and log whether Tim or Gaull & Co is named or cited.

## 7. Case study playbook

The layout is `src/layouts/CaseStudy.astro`. It takes four props: `title`, `description`, `eyebrow`, `facts`. Know what each one becomes:

| Prop | Where it shows up |
|---|---|
| `title` | The page `<title>` (Base.astro adds " · Timothy Gaull", 16 characters), the visible H1, the Article `headline`, and the og/twitter title |
| `description` | The meta description, the visible lede under the H1, the Article `description`, and the og/twitter description |
| `eyebrow` | The visible line "Work · {eyebrow}" above the H1, and the Article `about` |
| `facts` | The `<dl class="facts">` block under the lede |

### 7.1 Pick the searches first

- Pick **one primary search** from the cluster tables (section 3). Choose the cluster this project is the strongest proof for.
- Pick **two to four supporting searches**: one close variant, one owner-phrased problem, and one local phrase if the client is in Middle Tennessee.
- Example for Gunter Metals: primary "brand identity with AI". Supporting: "can AI design a logo for my business", "logo for metal fabrication", "Middle Tennessee metal fabrication", "AI-readable product catalog".
- Write the choice in a comment at the top of the `.astro` file, so the next editor knows what the page is for.

### 7.2 Title (`title` prop)

- **Keep it to about 44 characters** so the full title, with " · Timothy Gaull" added, stays at about 60 or less. Google truncates by pixel width, not a fixed count, and drops the end first. So if you must go to 45 to 50 characters, put the search phrase at the front and let the name get cut. ([Google: title links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link))
- **The tradeoff:** `title` is also the H1. A 44-character H1 is fine and matches the site's punchy style. If a story needs a longer H1, add an optional `seoTitle` prop in `CaseStudy.astro` that falls back to `title` (see quick win 2). Keep the two close in meaning, or Google may rewrite the title.
- **Formulas:**
  - `[Client]: [primary search phrase]`, as in "Gunter Metals: AI-designed brand identity" (41 / 57 full)
  - `[Primary phrase] for a [industry]`, as in "AI quote triage for a fabrication shop" (38 / 54)
  - `[Result] at [client]`, as in "AllianceBernstein: 6,000 people off paper" (41 / 57)
- No keyword lists, no pipes, no "Case study:" prefix (the eyebrow already says "Work").

### 7.3 Description (`description` prop)

- **150 to 160 characters.** Longer gets cut in results. ([Google: snippets](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet))
- It's also the visible lede, so write it as the story's first sentence, not as an ad.
- **Formula:** `[What was done] for [client], a [industry] in [place]. [How, in one clause]. [Result number if approved].`
- Include the primary search phrase or a close variant once, plus the place for local clients.
- Example (158 characters): "I designed Gunter Metals' brand identity with Claude in four days, then built the Middle Tennessee shop a storefront where customers order metal from a phone."
- If the lede needs to be longer than 160 characters, add an optional `metaDescription` prop (quick win 2) rather than overloading this one.

### 7.4 Eyebrow (`eyebrow` prop)

- Pattern: **`Client · industry`**, such as "Gunter Metals · metal fabrication" or "Pedro Pet Pals · animal rescue, volunteer".
- Keep it under about 45 characters. It adds the industry word near the top of the page.
- Because it also becomes Article `about`, keep the client name first.

### 7.5 Facts (`facts` prop)

- Three to five items, using these labels so case studies line up and AI engines can compare them:
  - **Industry**: "Metal fabrication"
  - **Location**: "Middle Tennessee" (or the town, if approved)
  - **Service**: one of the exact names "AI Opportunity Sprint", "Brand & Business Foundations" or "Fractional AI & Growth Partner", so the page ties to the service page
  - **Timeline**: "Brand in 4 days" or "2 weeks"
  - **Result**: one number with a unit, only if verified and approved
  - Optional: **Tools** ("Claude, Illustrator, Figma"), **Status** ("Live at guntermetals.com"), **Role** (for employer work)
- Values should be short and plain. No adjectives.

### 7.6 Body headings (H2s that answer real questions)

Default outline. Rename each H2 to the question an owner would ask:

1. **What was slowing [client] down?** (the situation, in the owner's terms)
2. **What we did** (or "How we [primary phrase]"). Name the service.
3. **What AI did, and what people decided.** Every Tim case study should say this. It's his difference, and it answers the trust question.
4. **What changed.** Numbers, before and after, and an approved owner quote.
5. **What this means for a [industry] owner.** Generalize the lesson and link the kit or service.
6. Optional: **Questions owners ask** (FAQ; see 7.10).

### 7.7 Where the primary search phrase should appear

Once in each place, naturally. Never repeat it to pad the page.
- `title` (near the start)
- `description` / lede (first sentence)
- One H2
- The first paragraph of the body
- One image's alt text, only if it truly describes the image
- The card on `src/pages/work/index.astro` and the line in `public/llms.txt`
- Link text from the service page that links to the case study

Keep the URL as `/work/client-name/`. Don't put keywords in slugs, and don't change slugs later.

### 7.8 Internal links

Every case study should link to:
- The matching **service page**, once those exist (`/ai-consulting/`, `/brand-strategy/` or `/fractional-ai/`)
- **`/kit/`**, in context, as Gunter Metals already does. The layout also adds a kit link in the closing note.
- **`/intro-call/`**. The layout adds this automatically.
- One related **case study** or **guide**
- `/nashville/` for Middle Tennessee clients, once it exists

Every case study should be linked from:
- `src/pages/work/index.astro` (the `cases` array)
- Its service page, in a "Work that shows it" section
- `src/pages/index.astro` (the `work` array) if it's one of the top three
- `public/llms.txt`

Use descriptive link text ("the AI-designed brand for a metal fabrication shop"), never "click here" or "read more" alone.

### 7.9 Images and alt text

- Alt text says what the image shows and why it matters, in one sentence. For example: "The Gunter Metals LLC wordmark in dark steel, with stencil bridges cut through the R and the A." The existing alt texts are good models.
- Purely decorative images get `alt=""`.
- Keep the `width`/`height` attributes and `loading="lazy"` below the first screen, as the current pages do.
- Use descriptive file names (`gunter-wordmark-hero.jpg` is right).
- Plan one 1200×630 social image per case study. The layout doesn't pass an image yet (quick win 3).

### 7.10 When to add an FAQ section

Add 3 to 5 questions when:
- the primary search is a learning question ("can AI design a logo?", "how long does an AI sprint take?"), or
- owners asked the same questions on intro calls, or
- the case study touches cost, time or tools.

Answer first, in 40 to 80 words. Reuse the kit page's `<details>` pattern. Note: Google stopped showing FAQ rich results in 2026, so FAQ schema won't change how the page looks in Google. The questions still help readers and AI answer engines. ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-drops-faq-rich-results-from-search/574429/))

### 7.11 Facts and permissions

- Use only facts Tim can verify, and numbers and quotes the client approved. The feedback form already promises "nothing goes public until you've read and approved the exact quote".
- If a client can't be named yet, describe them like the kit page does ("A fabrication shop") and keep the industry, place and result specific.

### 7.12 After publishing (every time)

1. Add the card to `src/pages/work/index.astro` (`cases`: `href`, `kind`, `stat`, `title`, `body`).
2. If it's a top-three story, update `work` in `src/pages/index.astro`.
3. Add one line under "## Case studies" in `public/llms.txt`: `- [Client](URL): what was done, for whom (industry, place), result.`
4. The sitemap updates itself (`@astrojs/sitemap` in `astro.config.mjs`). After deploy, check that the URL appears in `/sitemap-0.xml`.
5. In Search Console, run URL Inspection and click "Request indexing". In Bing Webmaster Tools, submit the URL (IndexNow covers this if Crawler Hints is on).
6. Post it on the Business Profile and LinkedIn.
7. Ask the client for a review, and for a link from their site if they're happy to give one.

The one-page version of this is `docs/case-study-seo-checklist.md`.

## 10. Measurement

### 10.1 Set-up (once)

- **Google Search Console.** Add a **Domain property** for `timothygaull.com`, verified with a DNS TXT record in Cloudflare. Submit `https://timothygaull.com/sitemap-index.xml`. Run URL Inspection on every page and request indexing. Optionally add `gaullco.com` as its own property, to confirm Google sees its 301 redirect.
- **Bing Webmaster Tools.** Use "Import from Google Search Console". It verifies the site and pulls in sitemaps. Watch the AI Performance report for Copilot citations. ([Bing: import from Search Console](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/september-2019/Import-sites-from-Search-Console-to-Bing-Webmaster-Tools), [BWT guide incl. AI Performance report](https://www.globalmediainsight.com/blog/bing-webmaster-tools/))
- **Cloudflare Crawler Hints** on, for IndexNow.
- **Google Business Profile performance** (built in).
- Optional and free: **Cloudflare Web Analytics**, if Tim wants page-level traffic; the site has no analytics today. Also optional: **Google Ads Keyword Planner**, with a free Ads account, for rough volume ranges that would replace the autocomplete-based demand estimates.
- The intro-call form already asks "How did you find me?", and `/admin/` shows the answers. That's the most honest lead-source data there is. Keep it.

### 10.2 What to track

| What | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Indexed pages vs. sitemap | Search Console › Pages; Bing › Site Explorer | Nothing ranks if it isn't indexed |
| Impressions, clicks and average position by query | Search Console › Performance (filter queries containing "nashville", "ai", "claude", "brand", "fractional") | Replaces every demand and competition estimate in section 3 |
| Same, by page | Search Console › Performance › Pages | Shows which case studies and service pages earn visibility |
| Business Profile searches, views, website clicks and calls | Business Profile › Performance | Local demand; which searches show the profile |
| Reviews (count, recency) | Business Profile, Clutch, Bing | Local ranking and AI trust |
| Copilot or Bing AI citations | Bing Webmaster Tools AI Performance | AI visibility you can measure |
| Lead source | `/admin/` intro-call answers; kit sign-ups | Whether search turns into conversations |
| AI answer presence | The monthly prompt test below | Whether AI engines name Tim |

Note: Google counts AI Overviews and AI Mode clicks in normal Search Console web results. They aren't broken out separately.

### 10.3 Monthly review (about 45 minutes, first week of each month)

1. **Search Console:** compare the last 28 days with the previous 28. List new queries with 10 or more impressions. List pages that lost position. Check Pages for anything "Discovered/Crawled – not indexed" and fix or re-request.
2. **Bing Webmaster Tools:** the same, plus the AI Performance report.
3. **Business Profile:** search views, website clicks and calls. Reply to any new review. Post one update.
4. **Leads:** tally "How did you find me?" (Google, Maps, ChatGPT or other AI, LinkedIn, referral) and kit sign-ups.
5. **Prompt test** (same six prompts every month, in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Mode, Claude and Copilot; record yes/no and the cited URL):
   - "Who is an AI consultant in Nashville for small businesses?"
   - "Who can help a metal fabrication shop use AI?"
   - "What is the AI Foundation Kit?"
   - "Can AI design a brand identity? Show an example."
   - "Fractional AI officer for a small company in Tennessee"
   - "Who is Timothy Gaull?"
6. **Decide one thing:** which cluster the next case study or guide targets, based on what's gaining impressions.

Every quarter, re-rank the clusters in section 3 using real Search Console impressions instead of the estimates. Upgrade or downgrade confidence. Re-run the autocomplete check below for new phrasing.

### 10.4 Re-running the demand check

A future session can repeat the autocomplete research. Request `https://suggestqueries.google.com/complete/search?client=firefox&hl=en&gl=us&q=QUERY`, which returns JSON. Compare the number and kind of suggestions with this document. Bing's equivalent is `https://api.bing.com/osjson.aspx?query=QUERY`. Both are rough signals of demand, never volumes.

## 11. Assumptions and uncertainty

- **No real volumes.** Autocomplete presence shows that people search something, not how many. Suggestions vary by location, and these were pulled with US settings from outside Nashville, so local phrasing may be under-counted.
- **The search tool used for this research isn't Google.** The results seen may differ from what Nashville users see, and map pack results weren't visible at all. A manual Google search from Nashville, logged out, would confirm the local picture.
- **Index status unknown.** Brand searches didn't return the site in the research tool, and a Bing `site:` check couldn't be read reliably. Search Console will settle it.
- **Blocked tools:** Reddit (direct and domain-filtered search both refused), Google Trends (HTTP 429) and DuckDuckGo (connection reset). Owner phrasing came from autocomplete, BiggerPockets, contractor publications and secondary write-ups of Reddit threads. The Jobber community forum returned 403.
- **AI-search studies** (Whitespark, MaxAEO, ThinkProfits and others) are mostly by vendors. Treat them as directional.
- **Product names change.** "Claude for Small Business" and "Cowork" are Anthropic's names as of 2026 and may change. Never imply a partnership, certification or endorsement.
- **Google Business Profile state is unknown** beyond "verified" (per the repo). Categories, reviews, photos and service areas weren't visible.
- **Facts Tim must supply before some recommendations can ship:**
  - a business phone and/or email for NAP
  - which towns he meets clients in person
  - price ranges or cost drivers (A5, B4)
  - whether Memphis in `areaServed` is intentional
  - Gunter Metals' town and permission to name it
  - any measured storefront results
  - client permissions for every named case study and quote
  - whether the anonymized "fabrication shop" and "global sales team" projects on the kit page can become named case studies
